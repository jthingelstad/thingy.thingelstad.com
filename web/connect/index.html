The Librarian MCP server

Connect your AI to Thingy

Bring Jamie Thingelstad’s whole archive into Claude, ChatGPT or Claude Code. Your AI gets the same tools Thingy uses: it can search, read, count and trace across twenty-five years of writing, and link you to every source it uses. Weekly Thing subscribers sign in with their email; there is no password.

Plug me in!

Building with it, or curious how it works? The Librarian MCP reference documents every tool with its parameters and results, the OAuth flow, budgets and protocol conventions, generated from the server’s own declarations.

Apps that ask for a client ID

Some apps don’t sign up on their own. Instead they show a callback (redirect) URL and ask you for a client ID, an authorization URL and a token URL. Sign in to Thingy, open your profile, and under MCP connections choose Set up an app. Paste the app’s callback URL there, and Thingy gives you every value to copy back: the client ID, the two URLs, the scope (archive:read), and the MCP server URL. Leave the client secret blank and turn PKCE (code challenge) on. A client ID you set up works only for your own account, and deleting it disconnects the app.

Agents in your browser (WebMCP, beta)

beta

While you are signed in on the Thingy chat page, the page also registers its archive tools with your browser’s model context (the emerging WebMCP standard). An agent working in your browser, such as Gemini in Chrome or a desktop MCP client connected through a WebMCP bridge extension, can then search the archive with your session and no connector setup. The tools appear when you sign in and go away when you sign out; they only read, and they see nothing the chat itself cannot.

House rules

Fair use

Each reader can make 500 tool calls a day (1,000 for Supporting Members) and up to 300 an hour. That budget is separate from Thingy’s own chat. A busy research session uses a few dozen calls.
